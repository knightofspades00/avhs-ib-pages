What IB really means for your child

The International Baccalaureate is the most respected high-school credential in the world. Universities recognize it on every continent. Employers know what it stands for. But the credential isn't the most important thing. Here's what your child will actually become:

  • A writer. An IB student writes constantly. By graduation, your child will have produced a 4,000-word independent research paper, a 1,600-word philosophy essay, and dozens of analytical essays in between.
  • A thinker. Not "smart" — analytical. They will know how to take apart a claim, find the evidence, and decide what they think for themselves.
  • A communicator in more than one language. Every IB student studies a second language for two years at the high-school level.
  • A person who serves. CAS (Creativity, Activity, Service) requires sustained engagement with their community over 18 months.
  • A self-manager. Long projects with multiple deadlines. They learn to plan, work in pieces, revise — without you reminding them.
The biggest thing you'll notice.

Your child will start arguing with you about ideas. Politely. With evidence. This is a good thing.

"How much homework is there?"

Honest answer: more than a typical high-school path. Typical IB Diploma students spend 2–3 hours per night on schoolwork, sometimes more during exam season or when a major project is due. Some weekends are work-heavy.

But here's the truth: volume of homework is not what makes IB hard. What makes it hard is the thinking required. An IB student isn't doing twice as many problems — they're being asked to explain, justify, and reflect on their work in a way that takes time.

Your job isn't to do the work, manage the work, or even check the work. Your job is to:

  • Create a study environment. A consistent place, predictable time, fewer interruptions.
  • Protect sleep. An IB student who gets 8 hours sleeps better than one who gets a higher score the next day on no sleep. Sleep is non-negotiable.
  • Ask good questions. "What are you working on?" "What's the hardest part?" "What does your teacher think?" Don't ask "Did you do your homework?"
  • Trust them. Trust them to figure it out. Trust the program. Trust that struggle isn't failure.

How to support an IB student

What helps

  • Quiet study time at home. Even 90 minutes uninterrupted, three nights a week, is enormous.
  • A consistent dinner conversation. "What's the most interesting thing you read today?" — better than checking PowerSchool every hour.
  • Showing up to events. Parent night, the IB college fair, exhibition night for the Personal Project. Your presence matters.
  • Modeling rest. Your kid watches how you handle stress. If you grind without sleep, they will too.
  • Asking the school for help early. If grades are slipping or your child seems overwhelmed, contact the Coordinator before midterms, not after.

What doesn't help

  • Editing essays for them. (Examiners can tell. And it doesn't help them grow.)
  • Pulling them out of CAS for "more study time." CAS is the part of IB that keeps them human.
  • Comparing their schedule to a friend's at a different school. Different paths, different metrics.
  • Comparing one Hawk to another. Their journey is theirs.
  • Catastrophizing one bad grade. One bad grade is data, not destiny.

"Will IB help with college?"

Yes — in several specific ways:

  • College admissions. Universities know IB rigor. The IB Diploma is one of the strongest credentials a high-school applicant can present, especially for selective and out-of-state schools.
  • College credit. The UC system awards credit for HL scores of 5 or higher in most subjects. The CSU system has similar policies. Many private universities accept IB credit liberally — sometimes a full semester or year of advanced standing.
  • College readiness. This is the underrated one. Hawks who do IB tend to find college easier than their peers — not because college is easy, but because they've already practiced the skills (independent research, long-form writing, time management, study habits) that derail most first-year students.
  • Scholarships. Some universities offer scholarships specifically tied to the IB Diploma. Always ask.

For details, see the college credit section of the assessment page.

Parent involvement at AVHS IB

You don't need to become a school volunteer to be a great IB parent. But there are real ways to plug in if you want to:

IB Parent Information Nights

Held a few times per year — typically before lottery applications open, before exams, and during the application cycle for new families. Spanish interpretation provided.

CAS support

If your workplace, church, or community organization can host service experiences or job-shadow days, the CAS Coordinator wants to talk to you.

Exhibition night

Every spring, MYP 10th graders present their Personal Projects, and DP 12th graders present their Extended Essay topics. Bring your family. Bring your neighbors.

Mentoring

If you have a career, a craft, or a story Hawks could learn from — let us know. We always need professionals to share with students considering a path you've walked.

Common worries (and honest answers)

Will IB stress out my child?

IB asks a lot. Some weeks are stressful. The honest answer is: students who have a steady study routine, sleep enough, and stay involved in CAS handle the stress fine. Students who try to do it all on willpower at 2 a.m. don't. Habit beats hustle in IB.

If your child is showing signs of chronic overwhelm — not eating, not sleeping, withdrawing — that's a signal to contact the Coordinator, not a sign that IB is the wrong fit. Adjustments can be made.

What if my child wants to drop the diploma mid-program?

It happens, and it's not a failure. Students who decide the full diploma isn't right for them typically shift to Course Candidate status — they still take IB courses and earn college credit for them, just without the full diploma core. Their transcript still reflects IB rigor.

What if my child has a learning difference?

IB has formal accommodations for documented learning differences — including extended time on exams, separate testing rooms, and approved assistive technology. Your child's accommodations from their IEP or 504 plan transfer into the IB context. Talk to the Coordinator and to the AVHS counseling office.

What if we're not sure my child is "smart enough" for IB?

This worry is more common than you'd think — and it's almost always wrong. IB isn't a club for "smart kids." It's a path for curious, hard-working kids. The single best predictor of IB success is willingness to revise, not raw academic talent.

If your child is curious, willing to put in effort, and supported at home — they can do this. Talk to the Coordinator. We've helped hundreds of families make this decision.

What if we don't speak English at home?

Many AVHS IB families don't. That doesn't disadvantage your child — in fact, growing up multilingual is academic strength. Your child may even be eligible for the IB Bilingual Diploma. Communications from the school can be provided in Spanish, and interpretation is available at parent meetings. Just ask.

What if my child works after school to help the family?

Many IB students work. The reality of IB at AVHS is that our students aren't insulated — they have real lives, real responsibilities, real families. The program respects that. Some accommodations are possible (course load, deadlines). Some hard conversations may need to happen (cutting back hours during exam season). Talk to the Coordinator early — we'll help you find the balance.

Para los Padres · En Español

Esta sección contiene información clave para las familias hispanohablantes. Para más información en español, comuníquese con la oficina del Programa IB de Arroyo Valley.

¿Qué es el IB?

El Bachillerato Internacional (IB) es un programa académico riguroso reconocido por universidades en todo el mundo. En Arroyo Valley High School, ofrecemos dos programas:

  • Programa de los Años Intermedios (MYP) — grados 9 y 10
  • Programa del Diploma (DP) — grados 11 y 12

¿Cuánto cuesta?

El programa IB en Arroyo Valley es gratuito. Es parte de la educación pública gratuita. Los libros, materiales y exámenes generalmente están cubiertos por el distrito. Si tiene preocupaciones sobre algún costo, hable con el Coordinador del IB — encontraremos una solución.

¿El IB ayuda con la universidad?

Sí. Las universidades de la Universidad de California (UC) y California State University (CSU) reconocen los puntajes del IB y ofrecen créditos universitarios por puntajes calificados (generalmente 5 o más en cursos de Nivel Superior). Muchas universidades privadas también aceptan créditos del IB.

¿Cómo puedo apoyar a mi hijo o hija?

  • Cree un lugar de estudio en casa. Un espacio tranquilo, a la misma hora cada día.
  • Proteja el sueño. Ocho horas de sueño son más importantes que una hora extra de estudio.
  • Haga preguntas buenas. "¿Qué leíste hoy que fue interesante?" — mejor que "¿Hiciste la tarea?"
  • Asista a las noches de información para padres. Hay interpretación al español.
  • Pida ayuda temprano. Si su hijo tiene problemas, hable con el Coordinador del IB antes de los exámenes, no después.

¿Y si hablamos español en casa?

¡Es una ventaja, no una desventaja! Ser bilingüe es fuerza académica. Su hijo puede ser elegible para el Diploma Bilingüe del IB, un reconocimiento adicional que aparece en su título. Las comunicaciones de la escuela pueden proporcionarse en español. Pregunte.

¿Es muy difícil el IB?

Sí, el IB es exigente. Pero no es imposible. Cientos de estudiantes de Arroyo Valley lo han completado — trabajando, cuidando a sus hermanos, jugando deportes, y aplicando a la universidad. Lo que más predice el éxito en el IB es: buenos hábitos de estudio, suficiente sueño, y el apoyo de la familia.

¿Y si mi hijo trabaja después de la escuela?

Muchos estudiantes del IB trabajan. Es la realidad de muchas familias en San Bernardino. Hay maneras de balancear el trabajo con el IB — hable con el Coordinador del IB temprano y juntos encontrarán el plan correcto.

¿Y si mi hijo tiene diferencias de aprendizaje (IEP / 504)?

El IB permite adaptaciones formales para estudiantes con diferencias de aprendizaje documentadas: tiempo extendido en los exámenes, salas separadas, tecnología asistiva aprobada, y más. Las adaptaciones de su IEP o plan 504 pueden transferirse al contexto del IB. Hable con el Coordinador y con el consejero de su hijo.

¿Cómo me comunico con la escuela?

Llame a Arroyo Valley High School al (909) 381-4295 y pida hablar con el Coordinador del IB. Si necesita asistencia en español, pídalo — tenemos personal e intérpretes disponibles.

Una promesa.

El programa IB en Arroyo Valley es para todos los Halcones — no importa el idioma que se hable en casa, no importa el código postal, no importa si los padres fueron a la universidad o no. Si su hijo está dispuesto a trabajar duro y a hacer preguntas, este programa es para él o ella. Y nosotros estamos aquí para apoyarles a ambos.
