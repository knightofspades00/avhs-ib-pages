Frequently asked questions

The 30 questions we get most often — from parents, from students, from middle-schoolers thinking about applying, and from transfers walking in mid-year. Honest answers.

The big picture

What is the IB?

The International Baccalaureate is a non-profit foundation founded in 1968 that designs internationally recognized academic programs for students ages 3 to 19. It's taught in 5,800+ schools across 160+ countries. Arroyo Valley is an authorized IB World School offering two of those programs: the Middle Years Programme (grades 9–10) and the Diploma Programme (grades 11–12).

Is IB the same as AP?

No. AP is a series of single-subject exams. IB can be that too (as a Course Candidate), or it can be a full diploma — a coordinated 4-year program with required components like TOK, the Extended Essay, and CAS. AVHS doesn't offer AP; we focus on the IB pathway.

Both are recognized by colleges. The IB Diploma is often considered uniquely rigorous because of the core requirements.

Why doesn't AVHS offer AP courses?

AVHS and Cajon High School are the two IB lottery schools in SBCUSD. We've made the institutional choice to focus on IB because it's a coherent diploma with strong critical-thinking, writing, and service components — not just a collection of exams. Students still earn college credit through IB the same way they would through AP.

Is IB only for the "smart kids"?

No. IB is for curious kids who are willing to put in effort and ask for help. There's no IQ cutoff. The strongest predictor of IB success is willingness to revise — not raw academic talent.

How is the IB Diploma graded?

Each of your six subjects is graded 1–7. That's 6 × 7 = 42 possible points. Add up to 3 bonus points from your TOK + EE performance, and the total possible is 45. You need 24 points and to complete the core (CAS, TOK essay, EE) to earn the full diploma. See the assessment page for details.

Applying to the IB at AVHS

How do I apply to AVHS for the IB program?

AVHS is an SBCUSD magnet school. Students from anywhere in the district can apply through the magnet lottery. The application window typically opens in the fall and closes in early spring. Visit the application page for current dates and steps, or contact the IB Coordinator directly.

Do I have to live in the AVHS attendance area?

No. Any SBCUSD student can apply to the IB program through the magnet lottery. Many of our students live outside the AVHS boundary.

What grades do I need to apply?

There's no strict GPA cutoff for the lottery. Stronger middle-school grades — especially in core academic subjects — make a transition into the MYP smoother, but the program is open to a range of students. If you're worried about fit, talk to the Coordinator.

Can I join IB mid-high-school?

Yes, but it gets harder the later you join. Transfers into 10th grade are common and manageable. Transfers into 11th grade are possible but require careful planning to make sure all DP subject sequences line up. Transfers in 12th grade typically can't pursue the full Diploma but can take individual IB courses as Course Candidates.

What if my child is in elementary or middle school — can we prepare?

The best preparation is broad and steady: read widely, write often, get strong in math, and stay curious. There's no specific "IB prep" track — we just want kids who arrive at 9th grade with a habit of asking questions and finishing what they start.

Workload and balance

How much homework will there be?

Typical DP students average 2–3 hours of homework per night. Some weekends are heavier, especially during IA season or EE deadlines. The MYP is less intensive — closer to standard high-school homework, but with more long-term projects.

Can my child still play sports?

Yes — many do. AVHS varsity rosters include plenty of IB Hawks. The trick is planning: build study habits before season starts, not in the middle of it.

Can my child work after school?

Yes. Many IB Hawks have jobs. The key is communication — with your manager and with your teachers. Light hours during exam season and EE deadlines. Some IB students even count parts of their job toward CAS, depending on the context.

What about extracurriculars?

Encouraged. CAS literally requires you to have a life outside of class — creative, physical, and service-based. AVHS has clubs, athletics, the arts, JROTC, ASB, and academic competitions. IB Hawks tend to be the most-involved students on campus, not the least.

What if my child is struggling with mental health?

Talk to the AVHS counseling office and the IB Coordinator. We can adjust workload expectations, support deadlines, and connect families with resources. Mental health matters; we'd rather help your student stay in the program with adjustments than push them to a crisis. If your child is in immediate distress, call or text 988.

Money, fees, and access

Are there any fees?

IB at Arroyo Valley is part of your free public education. Tuition is free. Textbooks and materials are provided. Exam fees are typically covered by the district. Some optional things (field trips, supplies for CAS) may carry small costs, but no Hawk should be excluded for cost reasons — talk to the Coordinator if cost is a concern.

What if our family can't afford a graphing calculator / lab supplies / etc.?

Ask. AVHS has loaner equipment, fee waivers, and supply programs for exactly these situations. No student should miss IB for the price of a TI-84.

What if we don't have internet at home?

Talk to the school counselor. SBCUSD and various community partners offer low-cost or free internet programs. Many students also use the library media center after school, the public library, or hotspots offered through the district.

Language, culture, and identity

We speak Spanish at home — is that a problem?

The opposite. Being bilingual is academic strength. Your child may be eligible for the IB Bilingual Diploma, an additional recognition on top of the regular IB Diploma. Many AVHS Hawks earn it. Communications and parent nights are available in Spanish.

What languages can my child study at AVHS?

Spanish is the primary IB language acquisition offering — at multiple levels (ab initio for beginners, B for ongoing learners, and potentially Spanish A literature for heritage speakers). Talk to the Coordinator about specific levels available this year.

What if my child has an IEP or 504 plan?

IB allows formal accommodations for documented learning differences — including extended exam time, separate testing rooms, and assistive technology. Your child's existing IEP or 504 accommodations can transfer into the IB context. Talk to the Coordinator and the counseling office.

What if my child is an English Learner?

Many Hawks come into IB while still building English fluency. The program accommodates this — and the IB framework itself values multilingualism. Talk to the Coordinator about supports available.

College and what comes after

Will IB help with college admissions?

Yes. The IB Diploma is one of the strongest credentials a high-school applicant can present, particularly for selective and out-of-state universities. Even Course Candidate status — taking some IB classes without the full diploma — signals rigor to admissions officers.

Does IB give college credit?

Yes. The UC and CSU systems award credit for HL scores of 5+. Many private universities give credit for both HL and SL scores. See the credit policy section.

What if my child wants to go to community college?

Most California community colleges accept IB credit. The skills built in IB — writing, time management, research — also make community college transfer applications stronger and the eventual four-year degree easier.

What if my child doesn't want to go to college at all?

That's a real path. IB still serves them — the diploma is recognized by employers, the military (especially for officer pipelines), and trade and technical schools. The discipline, communication, and problem-solving skills transfer everywhere.

Day-to-day logistics

Will my child be in a separate "IB cohort" or with the rest of the school?

Both. IB Hawks have many classes together (the IB cohort moves through subjects as a group), but they also share electives, lunch, sports, and clubs with the broader AVHS student body. It's not a school-within-a-school.

Will my child have time for electives?

The DP fills most of the schedule (six IB subjects + TOK), but Hawks usually have room for one or two electives across the two years. The MYP years (9–10) have more elective flexibility.

What does the IB Coordinator actually do?

The Coordinator is the central point of contact for families, the link between AVHS and the international IB office, and the person who tracks each student's path through the diploma. If you have a question, the Coordinator is the right first person to ask.

How do I contact the IB Coordinator?

Call AVHS at (909) 381-4295 and ask to speak with the IB Coordinator, or visit the contact page for direct email and a meeting request form.
