The Middle Years Programme

The MYP is where Hawks build the habits of mind they'll need for the Diploma Programme — and for the rest of their lives. Inquiry, real-world connections, and skills that travel across subjects.

What the MYP actually is

The IB Middle Years Programme is a five-year framework designed for students ages 11–16. At AVHS, we run the high-school portion of the MYP — grades 9 and 10 — preparing Hawks to step into the Diploma Programme in 11th grade.

The MYP isn't a separate set of classes. It runs through every academic subject in 9th and 10th grade. Your English class is an MYP English class. Your geometry class is an MYP math class. They all use the same framework — same big ideas, same skills, same kind of assessment.

The promise of the MYP

By the end of 10th grade, a Hawk in the MYP can write across the curriculum, work in teams across cultures, manage their own learning, and tackle a research project of their own design.

The 8 MYP subject groups

Every MYP student studies all eight subject groups every year. At AVHS, these line up with the standard 9th- and 10th-grade course load — plus the IB framework underneath.

1. Language & Literature

English. Reading, writing, speaking, and analysis at progressively deeper levels.

2. Language Acquisition

A second language. Spanish at AVHS — typically Spanish for Native Speakers or Spanish Language & Culture.

3. Individuals & Societies

World History, Geography, and the social sciences. Where Hawks become historians, not just learners of history.

4. Sciences

Biology, Chemistry, Physics. Lab inquiry, scientific argumentation, and the connection between science and ethics.

5. Mathematics

Algebra, Geometry, and into Advanced Algebra. Building procedural fluency and mathematical thinking.

6. The Arts

Visual Arts, Music, Theatre, or Media. Hawks practice creative process, not just creative products.

7. Physical & Health Education

Movement, wellness, and the science of the body. Lifelong physical literacy.

8. Design

The design cycle: inquire, develop, create, evaluate. Solving real problems with technology or hands-on engineering.

What this looks like on a transcript

From the outside, these still look like ordinary high-school courses (English 9, Biology, Algebra II, etc.) — and they fulfill all UC/CSU "a–g" requirements. The MYP framework is how they're taught, not a different course on the transcript.

Approaches to Learning (ATL)

The MYP names five clusters of transferable skills — and every teacher in the building reinforces them. By 10th grade, Hawks can name what they're working on and how they're getting better at it.

Thinking

Critical, creative, transfer. Analyzing arguments, generating ideas, applying skills across contexts.

Communication

Reading, writing, speaking, listening. Across languages and across media.

Social

Collaboration. Negotiation. Conflict. The skills of working with people who don't think like you.

Self-management

Organization. Time. Stress. Reflection. The skills nobody teaches but everybody needs.

Research

Information literacy and media literacy. Finding, evaluating, and using sources well.

Global Contexts

Every MYP unit is anchored to one of six Global Contexts — the lens through which the content matters in the world. These aren't ornaments; they shape the assessment and the project work.

Identities and relationships

Who are we? What shapes us — family, culture, beliefs, language, health?

Orientation in space and time

Where and when are we? Personal histories, civilizations, journeys, turning points.

Personal and cultural expression

How do we express ourselves? Beliefs, values, identities — through art, language, action.

Scientific and technical innovation

How do we understand the world? Discovery, communities, environments, ethics.

Globalization and sustainability

How is everything connected? Systems, markets, decisions, consequences.

Fairness and development

What does it mean to be human? Rights, responsibilities, opportunity, justice.

Service as Action

The MYP requires Service as Action: ongoing student engagement with their community. Not a checkbox volunteer log — but real, sustained involvement where students reflect on what they're learning about themselves and the world.

At AVHS, Service as Action takes many forms:

  • Tutoring younger siblings or neighbors
  • Translating for family members at appointments and meetings
  • Helping at the local food bank, church, or community center
  • Organizing events on campus that serve a wider audience
  • Caring for younger family members so a parent can work

That last one matters. We recognize that many Hawks already serve their families and communities every day — and that work counts. Service as Action is about reflecting on it, naming what you're learning, and growing through it.

The Personal Project (10th grade)

In the spring of 10th grade, every MYP student completes a Personal Project: an independent, student-chosen, year-long project that culminates in a product (something you make, do, or organize) and a written report.

The Personal Project is the MYP's capstone. It's also the warm-up for the Extended Essay in 12th grade. Students learn how to:

  • Pick a real goal that matters to them
  • Plan a multi-month process
  • Research what's already known
  • Build the thing — or do the thing
  • Reflect on what they learned and what they'd change

Past projects from MYP students nationwide include: writing and self-publishing a children's book in two languages; designing a community garden; producing a short documentary; coding an app; choreographing a dance performance; running a youth basketball clinic. The point isn't the topic — it's the discipline of carrying an idea all the way through.

For parents: this is when your 10th grader becomes a researcher.

You'll see them planning, drafting, revising — sometimes for months. Ask them to explain it to you. The reflection is half the project.

How MYP work is graded

MYP teachers grade student work against published criteria, not just a percentage. Every subject has four criteria (each scored 0–8), with specific descriptors for what each level looks like. By the end of the unit, students see exactly where they grew and where to focus next.

This is different from a typical American "85% B" grade. It's closer to: "On 'Analyzing,' you're at level 5 of 8 — here's what level 6 work looks like."

What about my child's GPA?

MYP work translates into traditional letter grades on the AVHS transcript and counts toward GPA exactly like any other class. The MYP framework is the method of teaching and assessing — the report card still looks familiar.

From MYP into the Diploma Programme

The MYP is intentionally built to feed the Diploma Programme. By the end of 10th grade, Hawks have already practiced:

  • Writing extended, sourced papers (warm-up for the Extended Essay)
  • Reflecting on service experiences (warm-up for CAS)
  • Working across disciplines (warm-up for TOK)
  • Sitting for criterion-based assessments (warm-up for IB exams)

That's the point. MYP doesn't just lead to DP — it makes DP feel like a continuation, not a cliff.
