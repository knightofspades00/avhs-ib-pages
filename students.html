So you're thinking about IB.

Or you're in it and trying to figure out how to survive — and thrive. Either way: this page is for you, not your parents. Honest answers, no sales pitch.

Is IB right for you?

Ask yourself these honestly. There are no wrong answers — just useful ones.

Are you curious?

Not "do you get A's" — do you actually wonder about stuff? Why things work the way they do? Why people do what they do? IB rewards curiosity more than raw smarts.

Are you willing to revise?

The biggest IB skill isn't being right the first time. It's being willing to redo a paper, redo a lab, rethink a position. If you hate revising — IB will feel painful.

Can you ask for help?

Hawks who do well in IB ask questions in class, go to teachers during lunch, and email when they're stuck. The ones who suffer try to figure it all out alone.

Do you care about something outside of school?

Art? Sports? Music? Your church? Your family? Your community? CAS is built for people who already have lives. If you've already got a thing — IB has a place for it.

If you answered "yeah, mostly" to most of these — IB is for you.

You don't need to be a straight-A genius. You don't need to come from a particular kind of family. You don't need to know what you want to be in college. You just need to be willing to show up.

A day in the life of an IB Diploma student

Every Hawk's day is different — but here's a typical one for a junior in the Diploma Programme:

TimeWhat's happening
6:30 a.m.Wake up. Breakfast. Quick check of any work due today.
7:30 a.m.School starts. Six IB classes throughout the day plus TOK.
LunchEither eat with friends or head to a teacher's room with a question.
3:00 p.m.School out. Maybe practice, maybe a club, maybe a CAS activity, maybe a job.
5:30–6:30 p.m.Dinner. Family time. (Yes, you still get this. Don't let IB take it.)
7:00–9:30 p.m.Homework. Two to three subjects per night, rotating. Big projects in the background.
10:00 p.m.Wind down. Phone away from the bed.
10:30 p.m.Sleep. Yes — actual sleep.
The all-nighter trap.

If you're pulling all-nighters in IB, something is broken — not because you're not "smart enough," but because the system isn't built for it. Talk to a teacher, talk to the Coordinator, and adjust before it cascades. Tired Hawks don't do their best work.

9 tips from Hawks who finished the diploma

1. Start the EE in the summer

Seriously. The Hawks who suffered with the Extended Essay senior year were the ones who waited. The ones who didn't suffer started reading in July.

2. Write your IAs the moment they're assigned

Internal Assessments are the part of your grade you control. They're not surprise tests — they're projects you can polish over weeks. Use the time.

3. CAS first, not last

Don't try to do CAS in March of senior year. Start in 11th grade. Find one thing you actually enjoy and stick with it.

4. Pick your supervisor carefully

Your EE supervisor is a teacher you'll work with closely for a year. Pick someone who'll push you, not just sign off on whatever.

5. Read the rubric before you start

Every IB assessment has a published rubric. Read it before you write. Most "5"s become "6"s by checking the rubric once.

6. Use your weekends

Not all weekend — but a few solid hours Saturday morning beats Sunday night panic every time.

7. Find your one quiet place

Library, your room, a specific corner of the kitchen — somewhere your brain knows means "work mode." It matters more than you think.

8. Don't compare to other Hawks

Some kids will finish their IAs in a weekend and brag. Some will procrastinate and panic. Run your own race. Your transcript is the one you have to live with.

9. Take the help

Teachers come early, stay late, answer emails, and run study sessions. Use them. They want you to win.

Balancing IB with everything else

Sports

Most varsity athletes at AVHS are in IB. Practice four nights a week, study after dinner, weekends for big projects. It works — but only if you're scheduled. Decide your study blocks before the season starts, not in the middle of October.

Work

Many Hawks work after school or weekends. That's reality. Two things help:

  • Communicate with your manager early. "I'm in IB. During exam weeks (April–May) and EE deadlines (winter of senior year) I'll need lighter hours." Most managers respect this if you give notice.
  • Use the bus, the lunch break, the slow shifts. Read assigned chapters at work breaks. Study flashcards on the bus. You'll find time other students don't have.

Family responsibilities

If you watch younger siblings, translate for your family, or contribute to the household — that's real, and it doesn't disqualify you from IB. The program respects students who have lives. Some of that work even counts toward CAS. Talk to the Coordinator. Don't try to hide your actual life.

Mental health

IB can be hard on mental health if you're not careful. Real talk:

  • Sleep is medicine. 8 hours minimum. No, you don't get more done by sleeping less. You get less done worse.
  • Movement matters. Even a 20-minute walk clears your head better than scrolling.
  • You can't pour from an empty cup. Take Sunday off if you need to. Your brain comes back stronger.
  • Talk to someone. AVHS counselors, your IB Coordinator, a trusted teacher. Asking for help isn't failure — it's strategy.
If you're really struggling.

If you're feeling overwhelmed, hopeless, or unsafe — reach out. Talk to a counselor at AVHS. Call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline). You matter more than any grade.

College, after IB

An IB Diploma is a strong college credential. Here's what to expect:

  • Apply broadly. UCs, CSUs, community colleges, private universities, out-of-state schools. The IB Diploma is recognized everywhere. Use that.
  • Tell the story. In your personal statement, you can talk about the EE, about CAS, about a moment in TOK that changed how you think. These are the kinds of stories admissions officers remember.
  • Get scholarships. Some schools have specific IB-Diploma scholarships. Always ask.
  • Use your credit. If you score 5+ on HL subjects, you can often skip introductory college classes — saving money and getting into your major faster. See credit policies.

Honest myth-busting

"IB will ruin my social life"

Wrong. Most IB Hawks have rich social lives. The work is real, but it's not 24/7. Cohort-based programs like AVHS's IB actually make it easier to build friendships — you're with the same kids in many classes over multiple years.

"AP is more impressive to colleges"

Both AP and IB are recognized. The IB Diploma — the full credential — is often considered more rigorous because of the core requirements (TOK, EE, CAS). Selective schools love seeing a strong IB Diploma.

"I won't have time for sports / drama / music"

Many Hawks do all of those during IB. It requires planning — not skipping. CAS even encourages your involvement.

"You have to be a math/science kid to do IB"

Nope. IB is built for humanities kids, STEM kids, arts kids, athletes, and undecided kids. Pick your HLs based on what you love, not what people expect.

"It's not worth it if I'm going to community college"

Most California community colleges accept IB credit. And the skills you build in IB — writing, time management, research — make community college transfer applications stronger and your eventual four-year degree easier.

If you want in

Talk to your counselor. Talk to the IB Coordinator. Come to a parent night with your family. Apply through the SBCUSD magnet lottery if you're not already at AVHS, or talk to your school counselor about joining the GLA pathway if you are.

One last thing.

If you're reading this and worrying that you're "not the kind of kid" who does IB — stop. The kids in IB at Arroyo Valley are the kids of San Bernardino. Bilingual. First-gen. Working part-time. Showing up. That is the kind of kid who does this. The Diploma was made for people like you, even if no one's told you that yet.
