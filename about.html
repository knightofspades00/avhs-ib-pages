About IB at AVHS

An IB World School — built for our students.

Arroyo Valley is an authorized IB World School offering both the Middle Years Programme and the Diploma Programme. Here's what that means, and why it matters for Hawks.

What is the International Baccalaureate?

The International Baccalaureate Organization (IBO) is a non-profit educational foundation, founded in 1968 in Geneva, Switzerland. It designs four programs for students ages 3–19, taught in roughly 5,800 schools across more than 160 countries. Two of those programs — the Middle Years Programme and the Diploma Programme — are taught at Arroyo Valley.

IB programs share a few things you won't always find together:

  • An international curriculum. The exact same Diploma Programme is taught in Tokyo, Toronto, Tegucigalpa, and San Bernardino. Hawks earn the same credential as their peers worldwide.
  • External assessment. Student work isn't just graded by the teacher — it's reviewed and moderated by examiners from around the world. That's why a "5" on an IB exam carries weight.
  • An emphasis on inquiry, not memorization. Hawks aren't trained to memorize answers. They're trained to ask better questions.
  • The IB Learner Profile. Ten attributes — like being principled, balanced, and reflective — that shape every class, every assignment.
  • The DP core. Theory of Knowledge, the Extended Essay, and CAS aren't add-ons. They're how IB students learn to think, write, and serve.

The IB Mission Statement

The International Baccalaureate aims to develop inquiring, knowledgeable and caring young people who help to create a better and more peaceful world through intercultural understanding and respect.

To this end the organization works with schools, governments and international organizations to develop challenging programmes of international education and rigorous assessment. These programmes encourage students across the world to become active, compassionate and lifelong learners who understand that other people, with their differences, can also be right.

Why Arroyo Valley?

The IB program at AVHS isn't a separate school-within-a-school. It runs through the school — touching every department, every academy, every Hawk who chooses to take it on. We're an IB World School, which means our teachers are trained in IB pedagogy, our curriculum is moderated against international standards, and our assessment is reviewed by IB examiners worldwide.

But IB at AVHS is also distinctly ours. Our students are predominantly first-generation college-bound. Many are multilingual. Many work, care for siblings, or contribute to the family income. The IB Diploma — with its focus on writing across the curriculum, second-language fluency, and service to community — is built for students like ours. Our families don't have to choose between college prep and staying connected to who they are. IB asks for both.

One of only two IB high schools in SBCUSD

Arroyo Valley High School and Cajon High School are the two IB lottery high schools in the San Bernardino City Unified School District. Both schools focus on the IB pathway — students take IB courses rather than Advanced Placement, with the option to earn an internationally recognized IB Diploma.

The Global Leadership Academy

At AVHS, the IB pathway is housed within the Global Leadership Academy (GLA) — one of several career-and-college academies on our campus, and a Linked Learning academy Silver-Certified for "Excellence in Holistic Leadership & Learning."

In the school's own words, the GLA's goal is to "encourage both personal development and academic achievement, challenging students to think critically, to ask the right questions, and think across disciplines." The academy delivers an IB education that "fosters diversity, curiosity, and a healthy appetite for learning" — and equips Hawks "with future-ready skills and prepares them to follow their career pathways, combining academic subjects with their own professional interests."

Joining the GLA at AVHS gives Hawks a structure for the whole journey:

  • A 9–10 cohort that takes MYP coursework together
  • An 11–12 cohort that takes DP coursework together
  • An IB Coordinator and counseling support specific to the program
  • Service learning and civic engagement woven into the curriculum, not added on
  • Access to college-credit IB exams in May of senior year

The two programs at AVHS, in one paragraph each

Grades 9–10

Middle Years Programme

The MYP is the foundation. Hawks take eight subject groups (language, language acquisition, individuals and societies, sciences, mathematics, arts, physical and health education, design). They develop Approaches-to-Learning skills, complete a Personal Project in 10th grade, and engage with Service as Action throughout. The MYP isn't a separate set of classes — it's how our 9th and 10th grade is taught.

Read more about the MYP →
Grades 11–12

Diploma Programme

The DP is two years of focused, college-level study. Hawks choose six subjects (one from each of the six groups, with three at Higher Level and three at Standard Level). They complete the DP core: Theory of Knowledge, the Extended Essay, and CAS. In May of senior year, they sit for external IB exams. Students can earn the full IB Diploma, or take individual IB courses as Course Candidates.

Our teaching philosophy

The IB doesn't dictate exactly how to teach — but it does require certain things to be true in the classroom. At AVHS, those things look like:

  • Concept-based learning. We don't just teach facts; we teach the big ideas (change, identity, systems) that make the facts cohere.
  • Inquiry as the default. Lessons start with a question, not an answer. Students do the cognitive work of figuring out, not just receiving.
  • Real-world relevance. Every unit ties to a Global Context or a local issue. Why does this matter outside the room?
  • Reflection, every cycle. Hawks don't just submit work — they reflect on what they learned, what they'd do differently, what's next.
  • Assessment for learning. Feedback isn't a grade; it's a conversation about what to try next.

"Is IB hard?"

Yes. We won't pretend otherwise. IB asks more of students than a typical high school path — more writing, more reflection, more thinking across subjects. The Extended Essay alone is a 4,000-word independent research project. CAS asks for 18 months of sustained creativity, activity, and service. Higher Level courses are taught at the level of a first-year university class.

But "hard" doesn't mean "for some kids and not others." Arroyo Valley students do this every year, in three languages, while playing varsity sports, working part-time, caring for siblings, and applying to college. The program is hard because the world is hard — and we don't apologize for preparing Hawks for it.
