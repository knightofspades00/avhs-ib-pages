Grades 11–12 · The Diploma

The IB Diploma Programme

Two years of focused, college-level study. Six subjects you choose. Three things at the core that change how you think — and a credential recognized by universities across the world.

The diploma in one paragraph

To earn the IB Diploma, Hawks complete six subjects over two years — three at Higher Level (HL) and three at Standard Level (SL). They take Theory of Knowledge, write a 4,000-word Extended Essay, and complete CAS (Creativity, Activity, Service) across the two years. In May of senior year, they sit for external IB exams. Subject scores (1–7) plus up to 3 bonus points from TOK/EE combine into a final score out of 45. 24 points is the minimum for the full diploma; 45 is perfect.

Higher Level vs Standard Level

Every subject is offered at two levels. The content is similar; the depth and contact hours are different.

Higher Level (HL)Standard Level (SL)
Hours over 2 years ~240 instructional hours ~150 instructional hours
Depth First-year university level Strong college-prep
You take how many? 3 HL subjects 3 SL subjects
College credit (UC/CSU) Generally awarded at 5+ Typically not eligible for credit
Choose HL in subjects you… are strongest in, or want to study in college want exposure to but aren't pursuing further

You choose HL strategically. A student who wants to be a nurse might pick HL Biology, HL Chemistry, and HL English. A student headed for business might pick HL Business Management, HL Math AA, and HL English. There's no "right" combination — your HLs should reflect your interests and your strongest disciplines.

How the 45 points work

Each of the six subjects is graded on a 1–7 scale:

  • 7 — Excellent
  • 6 — Very good
  • 5 — Good
  • 4 — Satisfactory
  • 3 — Mediocre
  • 2 — Poor
  • 1 — Very poor

That's 6 × 7 = 42 points from subjects. The remaining 3 bonus points come from the combined quality of Theory of Knowledge + the Extended Essay (the "TOK/EE matrix").

Total possible: 42 + 3 = 45 points

To earn the IB Diploma, students must score at least 24 points overall, complete CAS, and meet a few additional requirements (no failing conditions across subjects, satisfactory TOK and EE grades). Most universities consider 30+ a competitive score; 36+ is excellent; 40+ is exceptional.

Full Diploma vs Course Candidate

Not every Hawk pursues the full diploma. The IB offers two paths:

Full Diploma Candidate

Takes all six subjects (3 HL + 3 SL), completes TOK, EE, and CAS, and sits for all exams. Earns the IB Diploma. Eligible for maximum college credit.

  • Most demanding path
  • Strongest college application story
  • Eligible for the IB Bilingual Diploma if completing Language A in two languages

Course Candidate

Takes one or more IB courses and sits for those exams, but doesn't pursue the full diploma. Earns IB course certificates for each subject completed.

  • More flexibility
  • Still earns college credit for qualifying scores
  • Good fit for students who want IB depth in some subjects, not all

The IB exams (May of senior year)

In May of 12th grade, every DP student sits for external exams in each of their six subjects. Exams are written and graded by IB examiners worldwide — not by the AVHS teacher who taught the class. This is what makes IB scores universally trusted.

Exam formats vary by subject but typically include:

  • Paper 1: short-answer or stimulus-based
  • Paper 2: essay or structured response
  • Paper 3 (HL only): higher-order or extension
  • Plus an Internal Assessment (IA) — a major project or essay completed during the course, graded by the teacher and moderated by IB examiners

Roughly 20–30% of the final score in each subject comes from the IA. The rest comes from the May exams.

The IB Bilingual Diploma

Students who study Language A (literature/language) in two languages can earn the IB Bilingual Diploma — an additional recognition on top of the regular diploma. For many AVHS students who grew up speaking both English and Spanish, this is the credential that finally tells colleges what they've always known: that being bilingual is academic strength, not an asterisk.

Hawks: this matters.

If you grew up speaking Spanish at home and English at school, you've already done the hard work. The Bilingual Diploma is your transcript catching up to your real life.

What does an IB Diploma mean for college?

Universities understand IB. They've read thousands of IB transcripts. Here's what an IB Diploma signals:

  • You've done college-level work already. HL classes are first-year university courses. You've sat for external exams. You've written a 4,000-word research paper.
  • You can manage your own learning. CAS, EE, and TOK all require you to plan, execute, and reflect — without somebody else doing it for you.
  • You can write. The amount of writing in IB is unlike any other high-school program in the world.
  • You're more than a test score. CAS shows colleges that you serve, create, and stay active.

Specific college credit policies vary. See the assessment page for UC and CSU details.

A few myths to dismantle

"IB will hurt my GPA"

It won't. AVHS weights IB courses like other honors/advanced coursework. Your GPA reflects rigor, not just letters on a report card.

"IB is only for the 'smart kids'"

IB is for the curious kids — the ones who'd rather wrestle with a hard question than memorize an easy answer. There's no IQ test. There's a willingness test.

"IB won't help if I'm going to community college"

It will. Many California community colleges accept IB credit. And the skills you build — writing, time management, research — make any college path easier.

"AP is more recognized than IB"

Both are recognized. AP tests one subject at a time. The IB Diploma is a full credential. Many top universities prefer the IB Diploma because of the core requirements.
