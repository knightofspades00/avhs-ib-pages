Free study tools

Tools many IB students use. None are required by AVHS, and none are official IB products — but they're proven to help.

Khan Academy

Free practice across math, science, history, and more. Especially good for shoring up gaps before HL coursework.

khanacademy.org ↗

Crash Course

High-quality video crash courses on YouTube. World History, Biology, Chemistry, Psychology — all useful summaries for IB review.

thecrashcourse.com ↗

Purdue OWL (Writing)

The gold standard for citation, grammar, and academic writing. Essential for the EE and TOK essay.

owl.purdue.edu ↗

Quizlet

Flashcards for vocabulary, terms, and concepts. Many existing IB-subject decks. Free tier is fine.

quizlet.com ↗

Anki

Spaced-repetition flashcards. The serious-student tool. Steeper learning curve, huge payoff for languages, biology, history.

ankiweb.net ↗

Zotero / Mendeley

Free reference manager. Especially useful for the Extended Essay — track every source as you find it.

zotero.org ↗

A note on AI tools

You're going to use AI. Your teachers know that. Universities know that. The IB knows that. What matters is how you use it.

OK uses

  • Brainstorming a research question
  • Getting a concept explained a different way
  • Identifying gaps in your own draft
  • Practicing a foreign language
  • Generating practice problems

Not OK — will void your diploma

  • Submitting AI-generated text as your own writing
  • Using AI to "fix" your EE or TOK essay (substantive content must be yours)
  • Generating IA data or research
  • Submitting AI-translated work for language acquisition
  • Not disclosing AI use where required

The IB's official academic integrity policy includes specific guidance on AI use. Read the policy. When in doubt — ask your teacher first.
