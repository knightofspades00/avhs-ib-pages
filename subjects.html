DP Subjects · Grades 11–12

The subjects that build the diploma

Six subject groups. One subject from each. Three at Higher Level, three at Standard Level. Here's what each group is for — and what AVHS offers within it.

Course list updates each year.

Specific HL/SL availability depends on enrollment, staffing, and student demand. The lists below show what AVHS is most likely to offer. Confirm with the IB Coordinator before locking in your course choices.

How to choose your six

For the full diploma you take six subjects. The rules:

  • One subject from each of Groups 1–5 (Language A, Language Acquisition, Individuals & Societies, Sciences, Mathematics).
  • One subject from Group 6 (the Arts), OR a second subject from Groups 3 or 4. So you can replace the Arts with a second science, a second history, etc.
  • Three at Higher Level, three at Standard Level. HL subjects are deeper and meet more hours per week.
  • Pick HL where you're strongest or most interested. Future doctors lean HL Biology + HL Chemistry. Future humanities majors lean HL English + HL History.

Group 1 — Studies in Language & Literature

Your strongest language. For most Hawks, that's English. This is where you read deeply, write clearly, and learn to analyze texts of every kind — novels, poetry, film, speeches, advertisements, social-media discourse.

English A: Language & Literature — HL & SLHL SL

Note: students for whom Spanish is their dominant language may take Language A in Spanish through a school-supported self-taught option, opening the door to the IB Bilingual Diploma. Talk to the Coordinator.

Group 2 — Language Acquisition

A language other than your strongest one. Hawks typically take Spanish, with the level matched to the student's prior experience:

Spanish B — for students with 2–5 years of prior study or heritage exposureHL SL
Spanish ab initio — for true beginnersSL only
For Spanish-speaking families: your kid has options here.

If Spanish is spoken at home, Spanish B is usually the better fit — it builds on what you already have. Some heritage speakers may also be candidates for Spanish A Literature (a Group 1 course) and the IB Bilingual Diploma. The Coordinator can help you decide.

Group 3 — Individuals & Societies

The social sciences. Where Hawks learn to think like historians, psychologists, and economists. Reading primary sources. Building arguments. Interrogating data.

History — typically focused on 20th-century world historyHL SL
Psychology — biological, cognitive, sociocultural approachesHL SL
Business Management — strategy, finance, marketing, ethicsHL SL
Economics — micro, macro, global developmentHL SL

Group 4 — Sciences

Lab-based inquiry. Hawks design experiments, write up findings in scientific format, and engage with the ethics of scientific practice. Every Group 4 student also participates in the Group 4 Project — a collaborative interdisciplinary investigation with students from other sciences.

Biology — molecules to ecosystemsHL SL
Chemistry — quantitative, with significant lab workHL SL
Physics — mechanics, electromagnetism, modern physicsHL SL
Environmental Systems & Societies (ESS) — cross-listed with Group 3SL only
Sports, Exercise & Health Science — applied human physiologyHL SL
Tip: ESS counts double.

Environmental Systems & Societies is unique — it satisfies either Group 3 or Group 4. Students who want extra room in their schedule sometimes use ESS to free up another elective.

Group 5 — Mathematics

Every IB student takes mathematics. There are two distinct courses, and you choose based on the math you want to keep doing in college (not how "smart" you are).

Math: Analysis & Approaches (AA)

For students who love proofs and algebra. The path for engineering, physics, computer science, and pure mathematics in college.

HL SL

Math: Applications & Interpretation (AI)

For students who love modeling, statistics, and using math to make sense of the real world. The path for social sciences, business, design, and biology in college.

HL SL

Both courses are rigorous. AI isn't "easier math." It's a different kind of math. Pick the one that matches the work you want to do after high school.

Group 6 — The Arts (or a second from Groups 3/4)

This is the slot where you can either take an arts course or use it for a second subject from another group.

Arts options

Visual Arts — studio practice + exhibition + comparative studyHL SL
Theatre — performance, devising, theatre traditionsHL SL
Music — performance, composition, listeningHL SL
Film — production + textual analysis + comparative studyHL SL

Or instead of an art…

Pick a second course from Group 3 (a second history-or-social-science) or Group 4 (a second science). Common doubles:

  • Pre-med / health careers: Biology + Chemistry, both HL
  • Engineering: Physics HL + a second technical subject
  • Pre-law / political science: History HL + Psychology or Economics
  • Business / entrepreneurship: Business Management + Economics

And the DP core

On top of the six subjects, every full diploma candidate takes the DP core: Theory of Knowledge, the Extended Essay, and CAS. The core isn't optional — it's what makes IB different from any other diploma in the world.
