Assessment & College Credit

How IB grading actually works

The 1–7 scale. Internal vs External Assessment. The 45-point IB Diploma score. And — most importantly — how that score turns into college credit and college admissions.

The 1–7 grading scale

Every IB course is graded on a single scale: 1 to 7. That's it. No percentages, no letter grades — at least not from the IB. (Your AVHS report card will still show letter grades; the 1–7 is your IB score that goes to colleges.)

IB GradeDescriptorRoughly equivalent to…
7ExcellentAn exceptional A
6Very goodA strong A
5GoodAn A or B+ — and the typical cutoff for college credit
4SatisfactoryA B — passing IB, but typically not earning credit
3MediocreA C — passing the course, not the diploma threshold in many cases
2PoorA D
1Very poorAn F
The numbers are absolute, not relative.

IB scores are determined by what you can do, not where you rank against other students. A "6" in Tokyo means the same thing as a "6" in San Bernardino. That's why universities trust the score.

What makes up your IB score

Your final 1–7 in each subject combines two things:

Internal Assessment (IA) — ~20–30%

A major project, paper, oral presentation, lab report, or portfolio completed during the course. Graded by your AVHS teacher. Moderated by IB examiners worldwide — meaning the IB checks the grading to make sure it's consistent globally.

Examples: the History IA is a 2,200-word historical investigation. The Biology IA is an independent scientific investigation. English Language & Literature has an Individual Oral.

External Assessment (EA) — ~70–80%

The IB exams in May of senior year. Written by the IB, graded by trained examiners outside your school. Usually 2–3 papers per subject.

Papers vary by subject: short answer, structured questions, essay, data analysis, source analysis. HL students get an additional Paper 3 (extension/depth).

The IA is your safety net.

If a student bombs the May exam, a strong IA can still pull their score up. That's why we tell Hawks to take the IA seriously from day one — it's the part of your grade you have months to perfect.

The 45-point IB Diploma score

From subjects: up to 42 points

6 subjects × 7 points each = 42 maximum.

From TOK + EE: up to 3 bonus points

Your TOK essay and Extended Essay are each graded A through E. A "matrix" combines them into 0–3 bonus points.

Total possible: 45 points.

What counts as "passing" the diploma?

To earn the full IB Diploma, students need to:

  • Score at least 24 points total
  • Receive a passing grade in TOK and the Extended Essay (no E grades, and not two D's combined)
  • Complete CAS (the CAS Coordinator certifies completion)
  • Not score below 3 in more than one subject
  • Not score below 4 in more than two subjects
  • HL totals at least 12 points; SL totals at least 9 points

How score totals translate to college outcomes

Total scoreWhat it signals
24–28Diploma earned. Strong student.
29–34Solid, competitive — eligible at most public universities.
35–39Very strong — competitive at selective universities.
40–44Exceptional — competitive at highly selective universities.
45Perfect score. Worldwide, this is rare.

The May exam session

Every IB student worldwide sits for their externally-assessed exams during the same window each May. AVHS schedules its exam rooms, proctors, and accommodations during that window.

  • Exams run 2–5 hours. Multiple papers per subject across multiple days.
  • You can request accommodations if you have a documented learning difference (IEP, 504). Coordinator handles this.
  • Results are released in early July via the IB.
  • Score sending to universities is done through the IB's online system — your college will receive your scores directly.
Senior year hits differently in IB.

Most U.S. students "coast" senior year. IB seniors finish strong because the May exams matter. That said: there's life after the exam. Many AVHS seniors finish their EE and IAs by spring, then have weeks of actual rest before graduation.

College credit policies

This is the part parents and students always want to know. Here's the short version.

University of California (UC) system

The UC system awards credit for Higher Level subjects with scores of 5, 6, or 7. (Standard Level subjects typically don't earn credit.) Each campus has its own specifics, but generally:

  • Up to 3 quarter units (or 2 semester units) per HL subject earning 5+
  • Some HL subjects earn credit toward general education or major requirements
  • The full IB Diploma can also count toward UC eligibility for transfer students

Always check the specific UC campus's IB credit chart — UC's official IB credit page is updated annually.

California State University (CSU) system

The CSU system awards credit for HL scores of 5+ — typically 6 semester units per HL subject. Like UC, the specific applicability (gen-ed vs major) depends on the campus and subject.

See CSU's IB credit page for current details.

Private and out-of-state universities

Most private universities and many out-of-state schools award generous IB credit:

  • Many accept SL scores of 5+ for credit, in addition to HL.
  • Some give a full year of advanced standing for the full IB Diploma with a high score.
  • Some offer scholarships specifically tied to the IB Diploma.

Check each university's IB credit policy on their admissions page.

Community colleges

Most California community colleges accept IB credit (often more liberally than UC/CSU). If your path includes community college first, your IB scores still earn you placement and credit — and you can transfer to UC/CSU later with the credit intact.

The Bilingual Diploma — a credential AVHS families should know about

Students who complete Language A (their strongest language) in two languages earn the IB Bilingual Diploma — a separate recognition on top of the regular IB Diploma.

For AVHS Hawks who speak Spanish at home, this is often achievable through:

  • English A: Language & Literature
  • Spanish A: Literature (sometimes offered as a self-taught option with school support)

The Bilingual Diploma is recognized by universities worldwide and clearly signals fluency at an academic level. For students considering careers in healthcare, law, journalism, international business, or any field where bilingual professionals are in demand — it's a real differentiator.

Talk to the Coordinator early.

The Bilingual Diploma path needs to be planned in 10th or 11th grade. If it sounds interesting, raise it during course selection.

A note on MYP grading

The Middle Years Programme uses a different system — criterion-based grading. Each MYP subject has four criteria (typically scored 0–8), and final grades come from the descriptors associated with each level.

On the AVHS report card, MYP grades show up as regular letter grades. They count toward GPA exactly like any other class. The MYP criteria are the framework behind the grade.

Students who complete the MYP receive an MYP Certificate in 10th grade based on:

  • Subject grades across the 8 groups
  • The Personal Project
  • Service as Action engagement

Academic honesty

The IB takes academic honesty seriously. Plagiarism, AI-generated work submitted as your own, fabricated data on labs, or collusion on individual assignments can result in losing your diploma. Not exaggeration — the IB has processes for investigation, and they enforce them.

This isn't punishment for the sake of punishment. It's because the IB credential means something only if every IB student earned theirs honestly. AVHS will teach you citation, paraphrasing, and how to use AI tools ethically (because they exist and you'll use them in college and at work). Use the tools right and you're fine.
